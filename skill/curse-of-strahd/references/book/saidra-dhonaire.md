# Saidra d'Honaire and Dementlieu — published source
Source: Ravenloft: The Horrors Within (2026), Ch 2 "Dementlieu", pp. 70–76 (stat block p. 72); also pp. 4, 5, 7–8, 37–46, 187, 189, 196–204, 253, 284–286. Cross-checks against Curse of Strahd (2016). Layer: [BOOK]. Draft.

All citations are RHW unless marked CoS. Book text is paraphrased.

## At a glance
- [BOOK] Saidra is the Darklord of **Dementlieu**, the "Domain of Decadent Delusion". The domain is one city, **Port-a-Lucine**, plus fog-bound outskirts. Its genres are dark fantasy and psychological horror. Its hallmarks are masquerades, decadent aristocracy, social decay, illusions and impostor syndrome (p. 70).
- She is a phantom duchess. She is plainly undead, yet everyone flatters her. Her weekly **Grand Masquerade** is the height of high society. Guests who fail to charm her are denounced as impostors and reduced to ash (p. 70).
- Between balls she takes off her mask and stalks the streets as **the Red Death** (p. 70, p. 75).
- She is **CR 9** undead: 5,000 XP, or 5,900 XP in her lair (p. 72).
- The book adventure is **The Grand Masquerade**, for level 4 (p. 76; level list p. 4). There is also a three-stage campaign arc for levels 1–8 (p. 75).
- Tarokka: her **aligned card is the Charlatan** (Seven of Glyphs) and her **opposed card is the Seer** (p. 70, p. 189).
- **The book gives no direct link to Strahd or Barovia** (see "Links to Barovia and Strahd").
- Pronunciation: SAY-druh dee-ON-air; Dementlieu is DAY-mon-lee-oo (p. 4).

## Who she is
- [PLAYER, in Dementlieu] A masked duchess, obviously not alive. Servants and sycophants still praise her poise, beauty and costumes (p. 72).
- [DM] She knows she is dead and her life isn't her own. She ignores it for empty celebrations and a parody of the life she wanted (p. 72).
- [DM] She is overbearing, elitist and unworldly. She is terrified of being exposed as a fraud, and she turns that fear on others: she calls people liars on the slightest evidence or any hint of vulgarity (p. 72).
- [DM] Her greeting line welcomes guests as jewels of her city, then fixes on a flaw in one guest's clothes. That is the snobbery in one moment (p. 72).

### Her tale (p. 74) [DM]
- She grew up poor and was a bully. Her father told her she was secretly royal. When he remarried, her stepmother and stepsisters mocked and slighted her.
- Her father finally admitted she was only distantly related to nobility. She ran to her mother's grave, cursing. A **grandmotherly stranger** overheard her and offered any wish. On hearing it, the stranger cackled and dressed her in **rubies and a crimson gown**.
- She went to the masquerade for a new duke's coronation and charmed the court. At the **midnight bells** the rubies evaporated into magical poison. Everyone at the ball died. That was her murderous wish, and it left her within reach of the throne. The poison rose as a mist.
- She woke in a grand estate in Port-a-Lucine. The servants called her duchess. The poison had dissolved her body into deathly vapour. Nobody seemed to notice, so she embraced the fantasy.
- Reading note (not book text): it is a Cinderella story turned inside out. There are stepsisters, a godmother, a midnight ball, and a glass or gold shoe among the domain's Mist talismans.

### What she wants
- To keep the lie alive: the perfect duchess of a perfect society (p. 70, p. 72).
- To punish impostors, the vulgar, the poor and sick, critics of nobles, and anyone who reminds her of where she came from (p. 72, p. 75).
- [DM] To bury her past. Letters and rumours tell her that her simple, spiteful family lives somewhere beyond her city. Their confessions could expose her (p. 72).
- These match two of RHW's generic Darklord goals, "Maintain a lie" and "Suppress a secret" (p. 196). The match is our reading, not a book statement.

## Dementlieu (pp. 70–71)
- **Domain features** [PLAYER, in Dementlieu] (p. 71):
  - City of Lies: everyone pretends to be more successful than they are, and everyone knows it.
  - Grand Masquerade weekly: invitations are coveted, and invitees who stay away rarely survive long.
  - Lord Governor Marcel Guignol runs the city, advised by aristocrats, the duchess among them.
  - Masks are worn at social events and in daily life.
- **Mist talismans:** a jewelled or feathered mask, an article of well-worn fine clothing, or a shoe made of glass or gold (p. 70).
- **Chateaufaux** (p. 71). [PLAYER] Everyone talks about this countryside of noble summer estates and idyllic villages. [DM] It doesn't exist. Nobody travels far past the walls and no goods come in from outside. The Mists only show patches of forest and meadow. "Chateaufaux" explains anything that appears or disappears.
- **Locations** (table, p. 71). The [DM] parts are secrets.
  - Bonniville Museum of the Sea: its wolfish masthead is said to be cursed.
  - D'Honaire Estate: her lair (see below).
  - Great Library: a few rare tomes among hundreds of copies.
  - House of Wax: [DM] people replaced by wax constructs are imprisoned in the basements.
  - Lord Governor's Manor: said to be haunted by former rulers.
  - Mother of Tears Cathedral: its clergy of Ezra weep but do nothing.
  - Palace of Enlightenment: empty council chambers.
  - Phlegethan Hospital: [DM] a patient controls the doctors and claims to be the rightful Duke d'Honaire.
  - Opera House: crew vanish backstage.
  - Red Widow Theater: [DM] shape-shifting spiders.
  - Three Odd Gables: [DM] a coven of "godmotherly" green hags whose wish-contracts hide a catch.
  - University of Dementlieu.
  - Zuvich Hospital: Dr. Doloros Zuvich suspects the supernatural.
- **Other book mentions:**
  - The detectives Alanik Ray and Arthur Sedgwick live in Port-a-Lucine (p. 201).
  - The domain of Scaena broke off from Dementlieu when a playwright slaughtered his cast on stage (p. 187).

## Her domain powers (p. 74) [DM]
- **Closing the Borders:** she opens or closes the borders at will. While they are closed, "Chateaufaux" appears in the Mists. Travellers get lost among hedge-mazes and fog and come out on Port-a-Lucine's outskirts 2d6 hours later.
- **Empowered Actors:** a humanoid wearing a mask adds 1d8 to Charisma (Deception or Performance) checks.
- **Invitation:** at dawn she picks a creature whose name she knows or whom she has seen. A simple mask and an invitation to the next Grand Masquerade appear somewhere unobserved but obvious, such as a bedside table or among the target's gear.
- All three end if she dies and return when she re-forms.

## Lair: the d'Honaire Estate (p. 74; map 2.9, p. 76)
- It covers the largest island in Lucine Bay. The only ways in are the nobles' gondolas or larger ships (p. 71).
- It is brightly lit and full of expensive decor and art. A little of the art is good; most is middling.
- **Grand Entry:** a sweeping staircase to balconies. This is the ballroom.
- **Salons:** Skobeloff (a sunken ship), Sulfur (dizzying wallpaper), Taupe (underwhelming taxidermy).
- **Sea caves and docks:** gondolas moor in a sculpted cave, and stairs lead up to the house.
- **Second floor:** [DM] locked and guarded. Her own quarters are joyless and hollow, and she haunts them between balls and stalkings.
- [DM] The data gives no lair actions. In her lair her XP rises to 5,900, and inside her domain she gets four Legendary Resistances a day instead of three.

## Stat block: Saidra d'Honaire (p. 72) [DM]
- **Basics:**
  - Medium undead, neutral evil.
  - AC 13; HP 90 (12d8 + 36); speed 5 ft., fly 60 ft. (hover).
  - CR 9: 5,000 XP, or 5,900 XP in her lair.
- **Abilities and skills:**
  - Str 6, Dex 17, Con 16, Int 12, Wis 14, Cha 17.
  - Saves Dex +7, Wis +6.
  - Skills: Insight +10, Perception +6, Performance +7, Stealth +7.
  - Darkvision 60 ft.; passive Perception 16. Speaks Common.
  - Her initiative bonus includes double proficiency.
- **Defences:**
  - Resistant to acid, cold, fire, bludgeoning, piercing and slashing.
  - Immune to necrotic and poison.
  - Immune to the charmed, exhaustion, grappled, paralyzed, petrified, poisoned, prone, restrained and unconscious conditions.
- **Traits:**
  - *Darklord Restoration:* if killed, she revives at full HP 1d10 weeks later, somewhere in Dementlieu.
  - *Incorporeal Movement:* she takes 1d10 force damage if she ends her turn inside an object.
  - *Lie Detector:* she knows whenever she hears a lie.
  - *Legendary Resistance:* 3 a day, or 4 in her domain.
  - *Sunlight Sensitivity:* disadvantage on ability checks and attack rolls in sunlight.
- **Actions:**
  - *Multiattack:* three Necrotic Bursts. One can be swapped for a spell.
  - *Necrotic Burst:* +7 to hit, reach 5 ft. or range 30 ft.; 14 (2d10 + 3) necrotic damage.
  - *Life Drain:* one creature within 30 ft. makes a DC 15 Constitution save. On a failure it takes 28 (8d6) necrotic damage and its HP maximum drops by the same amount; on a success it takes half.
  - *Spellcasting* (Charisma, DC 15): *Charm Person* at will in duchess form only; *Fear* at will in Red Death form only.
  - *Truth or Die* (1/day): she asks a creature within 60 ft. to identify itself, then casts *Disintegrate* on it (DC 15). The target has disadvantage on the save if it lied.
- **Bonus action:** *Shape-Shift* between the duchess and the Red Death. Her statistics are the same in both forms.
- **Legendary actions:**
  - *Lash Out:* one Necrotic Burst.
  - *Shadow Veil:* she turns invisible until the start of her next turn and flies up to half her speed. The invisibility ends when she deals damage, and she can't use this twice in a row.
- **Notes:**
  - Her two forms mirror her two lives: she charms as the duchess and terrifies as the Red Death.
  - *Disintegrate* averages 75 force damage. One failed save can kill a mid-level character outright.
  - Her physical resistances don't cover radiant damage.

## Allies and servants
- The estate servants who hailed her as duchess (p. 74) and the flatterers around her (p. 72).
- **Ghoul servants** in formal wear and masks guard the door, fight unwelcome guests, and bring the dustpan after a disintegration (p. 76).
- **Political reach:** she is one of Lord Governor Guignol's advisers (p. 71).
- **Masquerade regulars** (p. 76). These are guests, not servants.
  - Alexandre du Cire: a Waxwork (CR 3, p. 277) who passes as human and runs the House of Wax. Friendly.
  - Angel Pajaro: a Werewolf opera diva. Hostile.
  - Julienne d'Honaire: a neutral good Bandit. Friendly. She claims to be the duchess's relative and wasn't invited; the book never confirms the kinship.
  - Lord Governor Guignol: a Noble. Indifferent, and wants to go home.
  - Raoul Rameau: a Guard Captain of the watch who makes bad guesses about who everyone is.

## Using her
- **As an enemy:**
  - The Red Death leaves life-drained corpses that locals ignore or scold (p. 75).
  - She hunts anyone investigating her past, so digging into her makes the characters targets.
  - Her anger can peak at the Grand Masquerade (arc, levels 6–8, p. 75).
  - In social scenes, *Truth or Die* and *Lie Detector* make lying to her deadly.
- **Defeating her:**
  - Killing her only sidelines her for 1d10 weeks (p. 72).
  - RHW's lasting defeats include "Truth": revealing the Darklord as a fraud or pretender (p. 198). Her family's confessions (p. 72) are the natural lever; that application is our inference.
- **As an ally or patron:**
  - The book gives her no specific ally role.
  - RHW's general "Darklord Patrons" rules apply (p. 199). A Darklord may test or employ characters, even send them into a rival's domain, and pay in escape, secrets, treasure or a Blessing (p. 200).
  - With Saidra, the price is performing to her standard without one lie.
  - At her ball, a Renown of 6 or more earns her compliment and noble contacts (p. 76).
- **At her own ball she does not fight.** Her ghouls do (p. 76).

## Adventure hooks in the book
- **Dementlieu campaign arc** (p. 75):
  - Levels 1–3: find a missing friend, performer or noble, and uncover disappearances caused by monsters hiding in plain sight (gargoyles, wererats, waxworks).
  - Levels 4–5: the trail leads to a noble, reachable only at the Grand Masquerade.
  - Levels 6–8: raid the noble's business (the opera house or the House of Wax) and find the missing turned into monsters. If the noble escapes, an invitation they can't refuse brings a showdown at the ball, and may draw the duchess's anger.
- **The Grand Masquerade** (p. 76, level 4). The date is public; the characters must make their own costumes and forge their own invitations.
  - *Dressed to Kill:* without a proper costume, a mask and 100 gp of jewellery (or magical equivalents), a character has disadvantage on Charisma checks. Armour can't be worn under a nonmagical costume. A nonmagical costume is ruined when its wearer becomes Bloodied.
  - *Arrival:* a forged invitation needs a DC 10 Charisma (Deception) check. Anyone who refuses to leave faces 4 masked **ghouls** (4 × 200 = 800 XP).
  - *Masquerade:* three tableaux of dancing, gossip and flirting. Each positive interaction adds +1 Renown per character per tableau. Recognising even a familiar masked face takes DC 10 Wisdom (Perception).
  - *Uncouth Exchange:* after the second tableau, two guests wearing the same costumes as two of the characters attack. They are **berserkers** (2 × 450 = 900 XP) using candelabras as greataxes. Winning adds +2 Renown.
  - *The Duchess Appears:* after the third tableau she drifts in, stops near the characters and denounces an "impostor". Julienne starts to answer and is disintegrated on the spot, and a ghoul sweeps up the ash. If the party spent two or more tableaux with Julienne, Renown drops by 1.
  - *Conclusion:* at Renown 5 or less, 4 ghouls attack and 2 more arrive every round until the party flees. At Renown 6 or more she compliments a costume, the target the characters came to meet approaches, offers follow, and each character earns 600 bonus XP.
  - All of the book's fights start below the Low budget for four level-4 characters (1,000 XP, 2024 DMG).
- **Other hooks:**
  - The Dementlieu Deceptions table (d6 liars, p. 74).
  - Every location on the p. 71 table.
  - [DM] Her family is somewhere beyond the city (p. 72).

## Tarokka
- **In RHW:**
  - Dementlieu's aligned card is the **Charlatan**: liars, and those who say they believe one thing but believe another (App., p. 286). Risibilos shares the card, so a random roll decides which domain it leads to (p. 189).
  - Dementlieu's opposed card is the **Seer**: inspiration and a keen mind, or a future that turns on a clever mind (p. 286).
- **Rules (p. 37):**
  - In the Mists, a tarokka deck can be used once every 24 hours. The card drawn works as a Mist talisman for the domain it is aligned with.
  - If a domain's opposed card is drawn in a reading inside that domain, its Darklord can look through the reader's eyes and speak through them for 1 minute. The reader doesn't remember it.
- **Barovia's cards:** aligned is the Darklord and opposed is the Mists (p. 38). In Barovia it is the **Mists** card, not the Seer, that lets Strahd look through a reader. This rule is new in RHW and is not in CoS.
- **The same cards in CoS (2016):**
  - The Charlatan as a treasure card means "a lonely mill on a precipice", which is Old Bonegrinder (CoS Ch 1, p. 14).
  - The Seer as the ally card means a dusk elf among the Vistani, Kasimir (p. 15).
  - The Seer as Strahd's location card means the Study, K37 (CoS Ch 1, p. 17; Ch 4, p. 66).

## Links to Barovia and Strahd
- **Direct links:** none. Her entry never mentions Strahd or Barovia, and his never mentions her.
- **Indirect hooks the book offers:**
  - *Roots of Evil* (p. 46): some Darklords may have first come out of the Mists in Barovia and met Strahd, and others want to break his bond with his land. This is general and the DM's choice.
  - Darklord rivalries and patrons: p. 192, p. 199.
  - **Madam Eva** (p. 253) is known across the Mists. She knows the tales and weaknesses of Darklords and when to enter the Mists, and she trades secrets for favours. What she learns, Strahd may soon know.
  - **Vistani** travel between domains (p. 204). In CoS, only Vistani pass Barovia's fog freely (CoS Ch 2, p. 27), and the gates open for anyone leaving only in Vistani company (Ch 2, area B, p. 33). Everyone else is turned back (Ch 2, p. 23).
  - Strahd opens and closes Barovia's borders at will (p. 40). A Mist talisman cannot get past closed borders (p. 7).
  - **Ez d'Avenir**, Van Richten's former pupil, roams the domains as a monster hunter (p. 201). In CoS her tarokka deck is hidden in her wagon (CoS Ch 1; Ch 11, area V1).
  - The **Keepers of the Feather** send trained ravens between domains (p. 203).
  - The Tatyana tables (p. 46) list Ireena as one incarnation (entry 3). One connection option is being "hidden from Strahd by another Darklord" (entry 2). This is generic, not Saidra.
- **Echoes only (the book makes no link):**
  - Her cackling wish-granter and Dementlieu's green-hag coven echo Barovia's hags and their dream pastries (p. 38, p. 71, p. 74).
  - Both Darklords were born from a massacre at a celebration: her midnight ball, and Strahd's wedding-day slaughter (p. 40, p. 74).
- **Sunlight:** Barovian daylight isn't sunlight (CoS Ch 2, p. 24). Her Sunlight Sensitivity would only trigger on magical sunlight there, such as the Sunsword's or the Holy Symbol of Ravenkind's.

## Secrets [DM]
- The Red Death is Saidra.
- Her humble origins, her stepfamily and the poisoned-ruby massacre (p. 74).
- Letters and rumours about her family beyond the city (p. 72).
- Chateaufaux doesn't exist, and closing the borders traps travellers inside it (pp. 71, 74).

- Du Cire is a waxwork and Angel Pajaro is a werewolf (p. 76).
- Invitations appear by her magic, at dawn (p. 74).

## Player-facing [PLAYER] — what anyone in Port-a-Lucine knows
- The duchess is undead, and nobody says so (p. 72).
- The Grand Masquerade happens weekly, its date is public, invitations are prized, and skipping one is dangerous (pp. 71, 76).
- Masks are everywhere; the Lord Governor rules; people talk about Chateaufaux (p. 71).
- "The Red Death" gets the blame for drained bodies (p. 75).
- Everyone lies about how well they are doing (p. 71).
