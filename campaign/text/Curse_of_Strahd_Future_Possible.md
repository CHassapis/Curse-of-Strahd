How to adjust any fight:
- They won the last one in two rounds: add one more of the main enemy (a spawn, a druid, a werewolf), or give the boss maximum HP.
- Someone dropped in round one: take one enemy away, or have the boss leave at half HP.
- A big fight is too much: split it into two waves instead of cutting it.
- Never two High fights without a long rest between them.
- The brides have their own scale — the leash, on page 4 of the Brides sheet.

### CASTLE RAVENLOFT — DINNER WITH STRAHD (EARLY VISIT)
When: at dusk on the day after the Festival. It's the invitation inside Strahd's letter, which they've been carrying unopened; the carriage comes whether or not they've opened it. Level 6.

#### The letter

| READ ALOUD The wax seal is a black raven. The paper is heavy and very old, the handwriting beautiful. "My dear guests. You have toured my valley with such energy that I would be a poor host not to receive you properly. Dine with me. A carriage will wait for you at dusk. You have my word that no harm will come to you under my roof tonight. — S." |
|---|


| HANDOUT 1 — Strahd's letter The printed letter, folded inside its wrapper with the black raven seal. They've carried it since Death House; when they break the seal in the game, they break it at the table. |
|---|

However early they open it, the carriage comes at dusk on the day after the Festival: Strahd wants to see what they do in Vallaki first. It waits outside the town gate, and the guards open the gate for it without being asked. That's the one night Vallaki's gate opens after dark.

#### The carriage

| READ ALOUD At dusk, on the road, a black carriage waits with its door open. Two black horses, steaming in the cold. No driver. Inside, red velvet, and a warm fur blanket folded on each seat. When the last of you steps in, the door closes on its own and the horses start to walk. |
|---|

- If they refuse to get in: the carriage waits all night. In the morning it's gone, and a single black rose lies on the road. He'll ask again, less politely.

#### The castle

| READ ALOUD The road climbs through the clouds. A bridge over a drop so deep the bottom is only mist. The gates are open, and the great doors too. Inside, torches light themselves as you pass. From somewhere deep in the castle comes organ music — slow, grand and very sad. |
|---|


| READ ALOUD (the dining hall) A long hall with a table set for one more than your number. Crystal, silver, candles. Roast meats, bread, fruit, wine. At the far end, an enormous organ, and a man at it with his back to you, playing. He finishes the piece. Then he turns. "Please. Sit. Eat. You are my guests." |
|---|


#### The dinner
- He doesn't eat. He watches them eat and asks questions — charming, curious, well informed.
- He knows everything: the mill, the hags, the inn fire, the Festival, which side they took.
- Rahadin, his chamberlain — a cold-eyed dusk elf — pours the wine and says nothing.
- The offer, again: "You've seen my valley now. You know what it costs to fight me. Bring me Ireena, and the mists open. The offer won't improve."
- To Cassian: "Your grandfather sat in that chair. He was the only one who ever laughed at my jokes."
- To Ireena (if she's there): he asks her to dance. "One dance. I promised you one, a very long time ago."

- If a bride was destroyed in Vallaki: her chair is empty, and her place is still laid. Strahd doesn't mention it. The other two do.
- If they explore after dinner: the promise is over. The brides come looking, one at a time, holding back — but they don't stop after two rounds. At level 6 this is the most dangerous thing in this document.

#### The rules
- Nobody is harmed during dinner. He gave his word.
- After dinner he lets them leave — or explore. If they explore, the promise is over; use the castle as written.
- If they attack him at the table: a demonstration, not a fight. Then the carriage takes them back, and next time he isn't a host.

### ON THE ROAD WEST — VOLENTA'S HUNT
Level 7 · one night, somewhere between the winery, Yester Hill and Van Richten's Tower
What happens: Volenta was told to stay home. She didn't. She follows the party for three nights — a giggle in the trees, a dead crow on a tent, a boot gone in the morning — and on the fourth night she comes in. (If Volenta is already destroyed, skip this.)

| READ ALOUD (the first night) Somewhere in the trees behind your camp, someone giggles. When you look, there's nothing there. In the morning, one of your boots is gone. |
|---|


| READ ALOUD (the fourth night) The fire gutters. On the lowest branch of the pine above you, upside down, a small figure in a brown gown hangs by her knees, grinning down through a white skull mask. "Hello again," she says. "Nobody's watching. Isn't that nice?" |

How strong: full stat block (level 7–9 on the leash): three Claws or two and a Bite, Pounce, and the Mourning Amulet (unless it broke in Vallaki). She leaves at half HP (75) with an escape roll, screaming "I'm telling!" 5,000 XP, or 2,500 if she gets away. Moderate at level 7.
What she says:
- "Run. Please run. It's so much better when you run."
- To whoever hurt her in Vallaki: "You! I kept you for last."
- Over someone at 1 HP: "Not yet. Not yet. Get up."

Afterwards: the next dusk, a raven lands with a note in Strahd's hand: "My wife has been punished. You have my apologies. — S." That night, carried on the wind from the castle, they hear screaming. He isn't sorry. He wants them to know she disobeyed.

### MOUNT BARATOK — THE MAD MAGE (optional)
Level 8+ · half a session · near Van Richten's Tower

#### Who he is
A legendary wizard — Mordenkainen — who came to Barovia to destroy Strahd, lost, and lost his mind. He lives alone in a ruined hut on the mountain, talking to himself, casting powerful spells at anything that frightens him.

#### What they'll see

| READ ALOUD Near the top of the mountain, a ruined stone hut with smoke from the chimney. Around it, the snow is scorched in perfect circles. An old man with a wild white beard stands outside in his nightshirt, lecturing a boulder. When he sees you, he stops. "You're late," he says. "No — you're early. Which one of you is the spy?" |
|---|


#### What he says
- "I came here to kill him, you know. I was very good. I was the best. Then he did something to my head."
- "The raven knows. Ask the raven." (The Keepers of the Feather.)
- "Don't look at the castle. It looks back."

#### How to handle him
- Don't fight him. He's far beyond the party. Attacked, he blasts, teleports away, and turns up later.
- Restore his mind: Greater Restoration, a vestige's gift at the Amber Temple, or a rare remedy from the Keepers of the Feather.
- Restored, he's a powerful ally — for one decisive moment at the castle. He won't stay, but he'll owe them.

#### Possible results

| IF… | THEN |
|---|---|
| They restore his mind | A powerful ally at the castle, once. He tells them about the Heart of Sorrow. |
| They leave him | He turns up at random, raving, at the worst moments. |
| They attack him | Heavy damage, and he vanishes. |

#### Events
The wedding dress. The Abbot has built Vasilka — a flesh golem stitched from corpses — to be Strahd's bride. He asks the party to find her a proper wedding dress.
- The Abbot: "A bride must look like a bride. Bring me a wedding dress, and I'll give you whatever you ask. Within reason."
- Where to find one: Lady Wachter (who has her own), the Baron's wife Lydia, or a tailor in Vallaki.
- If they bring it: the Abbot is delighted, opens the Abbey to them, and hands over the Holy Symbol of Ravenkind if they ask for it.

#### Who she is
