### D-1 — Ethradir and the dream pastries

**BOOK FACT.** Ch 6, Dream Pastries (p. 125). Eating a whole pastry calls for a DC 16 Constitution save. On a failure the eater falls into a **trance** for 1d4 + 4 hours: incapacitated, speed 0. It ends on any damage, or when someone uses an action to shake them. It's a dream-trance, not sleep. The 2024 elf's Trance says magic can't put an elf *to sleep*, so it doesn't obviously cover a trance.

**CURRENT** (the no-save sleep is a deliberate campaign choice, and reader 2 advises keeping it; the only open question is Ethradir):
- Scene 1, the pastries: `The pastries. Two dozen Dream Pastries. Anyone who eats one sleeps for 8 hours and can't be woken except by damage or an ally using an action to shake them.`
- Scene 4: `The pastries are Dream Pastries. Anyone who eats one starts Scene 5 asleep and can only be woken by damage or an ally using an action.`
- Scene 4: `- Ethradir is an elf: Trance means magic can't put him to sleep, so the pastry only tastes wonderful. He doesn't sleep at all, and he keeps his awareness while he trances. At midnight he rolls Perception as if he were on watch.`
- Combat sheet, Before it starts: `Ethradir can't be put to sleep (Trance) and stays aware: he rolls as on watch.`

**Variant A (keep):** no change. Ethradir is immune.

**Variant B (the book's trance; still no save for anyone):**
- Scene 4, the Ethradir bullet becomes:
  `- Ethradir is an elf, but this isn't sleep: the bone-flour in the pastry pulls you into a dream-trance, and an elf's Trance doesn't stop it. If he eats one, he starts Scene 5 under like anyone else. If he doesn't, he rolls Perception at midnight as if he were on watch.`
